6. What We Offer — Explore Our Content Services
Creative content covers ten specialized writing services, each built for a different business goal — from ranking a blog post to converting a product page. Every service follows the same core process: research, entity mapping, drafting, and SEO/AEO review, but the deliverables and structure change based on where the content will live.
Below is a breakdown of each service, what it solves, and who it's built for.
6.1 Content Writing
Content writing is the foundation service covering website pages, landing pages, and core brand copy that needs to explain what a business does clearly and persuasively. It typically includes homepage copy, about pages, and service pages, and is best for businesses launching a new site or repositioning an existing brand. Unlike one-off blog posts, content writing focuses on the pages a visitor reads before deciding to trust a business at all. A common mistake is writing this copy internally without keyword research, which means the page reads fine but never gets found. Explore Content Writing Services →
6.2 Article Writing
Article writing produces long-form, research-backed pieces — typically 1,000 to 2,500 words — built to establish topical authority on a subject. It suits businesses that want to rank for competitive, high-intent search terms rather than just fill a blog calendar. Articles differ from blog posts in depth: they're structured around a single core question, backed with more thorough research, and often used as pillar content that shorter posts link back to. This format works best when a business has 5–10 core topics it wants to own in search results, not for quick updates or announcements. 
Explore Article Writing Services →
6.3 Blog Writing
Blog writing delivers regular, shorter-form posts — usually 600 to 1,200 words — that keep a website active and answer specific, often narrower questions than a full article. It's best for businesses that want a consistent publishing cadence to support ongoing SEO and social sharing. Blog posts work well for how-to guides, industry updates, and list-based content that AI Overviews and featured snippets frequently pull from. A blog without a topic cluster strategy tends to plateau after the first few months, since posts stop reinforcing each other. 
Explore Blog Writing Services →
6.4 Marketing Content
Marketing content includes campaign copy, ad landing pages, email sequences, and promotional material tied to a specific offer or launch window. It's built for businesses running paid campaigns or product launches that need copy aligned tightly with a conversion goal rather than long-term SEO. This differs from evergreen content writing in that marketing content is time-bound and tested — headlines and CTAs are written to be measured against performance data, not just published once. It fits best alongside an active paid advertising or product launch effort. 
Explore Marketing Content Services →
6.5 Product Description Writing
Product description writing turns technical specs and features into copy that helps online shoppers decide to buy, typically 50 to 300 words per product, depending on catalog complexity. It's essential for e-commerce brands with catalogs ranging from a handful of SKUs to several thousand. Good product descriptions answer the buyer's unspoken objections — durability, sizing, use case — rather than just listing specifications, which is where many store owners fall short when writing these themselves. This service is not a fit for single-product or portfolio-style sites, where longer content writing serves better. 
Explore Product Description Services →
6.6 Press Release Writing
Press release writing produces newsworthy announcements — product launches, funding news, partnerships, milestones — formatted to meet journalistic and distribution-service standards. It's built for businesses with an actual news event to report, not general promotional updates, which media outlets and distribution platforms typically reject. A well-structured press release leads with the most newsworthy fact in the first sentence, includes verifiable quotes, and avoids sounding like an ad. Businesses without a distribution plan should pair this service with a distribution or PR outreach step, since writing alone doesn't guarantee pickup. 
Explore Press Release Services →
6.7 Email Newsletter Writing
Email newsletter writing creates recurring email content — product updates, curated resources, or nurture sequences — sent directly to a business's subscriber list. It suits businesses that already have an email list and want to keep it engaged between purchases or sales conversations. Newsletters differ from marketing email blasts in tone: they're built for retention and relationship, with softer CTAs and more editorial structure, rather than a single hard sell. This service assumes an existing list; businesses starting from zero subscribers should prioritize lead generation first. 
Explore Email Newsletter Services →
6.8 Product Review Writing
Product review writing creates detailed, comparison-style content evaluating products or services, often used for affiliate sites, review blogs, or brand comparison pages. It's built for businesses in affiliate marketing, SaaS comparison spaces, or any brand that wants to rank for "best X for Y" style searches. This format requires more structural discipline than a blog post — pros/cons, comparison tables, and a clear verdict — since both readers and AI Overviews extract these sections directly. It isn't the right fit for single-product brand pages, where product description writing applies instead. 
Explore Product Review Services →
6.9 Web Content Writing
Web content writing covers the full site experience — navigation copy, microcopy, FAQ sections, and supporting pages that tie a website together beyond the homepage and service pages. It's best for businesses mid-way through a website rebuild or redesign that need every page to speak with one consistent voice. This differs from general content writing in scope: it treats the whole site as one connected system rather than writing pages in isolation, which is often where DIY website copy breaks down. It pairs closely with web design and development work already underway. 
Explore Web Content Services →
6.10 Social Media Content Writing
Social media content writing produces platform-specific captions, post copy, and short-form scripts for Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn, tailored to each platform's tone and format. It's built for businesses running organic social alongside paid campaigns that need a consistent posting rhythm without writing every caption manually. Unlike blog or article writing, this content is written for scroll-speed attention — first line hooks, short sentences, and platform-native phrasing — rather than search ranking. This service works best paired with an existing content calendar or paid social strategy, not as a standalone effort.

7. Why Creative Content Matters
Generic or inconsistent content creates a specific, measurable risk: pages that don't match search intent get buried, and AI answer engines skip over content that isn't structured to be extracted. A business publishing content without a clear format strategy often ends up with a blog nobody reads, product pages that don't convert, and a brand voice that shifts from page to page.
The benefit of a structured content system is compounding — a blog post that links to a pillar article, which links to a product page, builds topical authority faster than any single piece written alone. This matters more now that Google AI Overviews and tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity summarize entire categories of content in one answer, often citing only the sources that are clearly structured. Businesses don't need more content — they need content mapped to the right format for the job it's meant to do.

9. Our Process
Every content project moves through the same six steps, regardless of format, though the depth of each step scales with the service.
1. Discovery & brief — Goals, target audience, focus keyword, and brand tone are gathered before writing starts.
2. Keyword & entity mapping — Related keywords and topic entities are identified so the content connects naturally to search intent.
3. Drafting — A writer matched to the content format and industry produces the first draft.
4. SEO/AEO review — Drafts are checked against search intent, readability, and AI-Overview structure before delivery.
5. Delivery & revision — Content is delivered with a revision window to align tone and detail with the brief.
6. Ongoing calendar (optional) — For blog, social, and newsletter formats, content can be planned on a recurring schedule rather than one-off orders.

14. FAQs
What's the difference between content writing and copywriting?
Content writing typically refers to informational or brand-building content like blog posts, articles, and website pages, while copywriting focuses on short, conversion-driven text like ads and CTAs. Riha Web Tech's content writing services cover both ends, depending on the format — marketing content, for example, leans closer to copywriting than a long-form article.
Do you write in both Bangla and English?
Yes. Content is produced in Bangla, English, or a bilingual mix depending on the target audience, which is common for Bangladeshi businesses serving both local and international customers from the same website.
How is AI used in your content process?
AI tools support research and structure checks, but drafts are written and reviewed by a person to ensure accuracy, brand voice, and factual correctness before delivery. This keeps content readable for humans while still being structured for AI answer engines to extract correctly.
Which service do I need for an e-commerce store?
Most e-commerce stores need a mix of product description writing for catalog pages and blog or article writing to drive organic traffic. Product review writing is also useful for comparison-style content if the store competes on "best X" search terms.
Can you match our existing brand voice?
Yes. The discovery step includes reviewing any existing brand guidelines, past content, or tone preferences so new content stays consistent with what's already published.
Do you write for industries you haven't worked in before?
Yes, though the discovery step for a new industry typically includes additional research time to understand terminology, audience concerns, and competitor positioning before drafting begins.
