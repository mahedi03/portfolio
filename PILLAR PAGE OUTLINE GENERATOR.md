PILLAR-SPECIFIC
🧩 Pillar vs. Cluster Role Check
Before outlining, confirm the division of labor between this pillar page and its sub-pillars:
✅  The pillar page introduces and scopes each sub-service at a high level — it does not duplicate the depth of the sub-pillar pages
✅  Every claim or process detail that belongs to one specific sub-service should live on that sub-pillar page, with the pillar page linking to it rather than explaining it in full
✅  The pillar page's own unique content is the material that ties the sub-services together: the overall methodology, the reason a client needs the umbrella service, and how the sub-services work as a system
💡  This prevents the common failure mode of pillar pages duplicating cluster content, which dilutes both pages' ability to rank and be cited independently.
🔍 Search Intent Analysis
First identify:
•	Primary search intent (usually commercial/service-research intent for pillar pages)
•	Secondary search intent
•	Reader's stage in the buying journey (aware of problem vs. comparing vendors vs. ready to hire)
•	What the reader expects to find on a page like this
•	What type of page Google and AI systems are likely rewarding for this query (service page vs. blog vs. directory)
💡 Content Angle
Suggest a unique positioning angle for this pillar page. Explain:
•	Why this pillar page should exist as a hub rather than just a list of services
•	How it will differ from generic agency service pages
•	What unique value, proof, or information gain it will provide

Proprietary Data / Proof Opportunity Flag
Pillar pages benefit most from proof elements a competitor cannot copy — real results, real process, real specificity.

🔬 Competitor Positioning Gap Analysis
Using the competitor pillar/service URLs provided, identify:
•	2-3 scope gaps — sub-services or deliverables competitors bundle or omit that this page should position differently
•	2 structural gaps — formatting or trust elements missing from competitor pages (e.g. no clear process, no pricing transparency, no comparison table)
•	1 trust gap — where competitors fail on E-E-A-T (no case studies, no team credentials, no clear service area)
💡  Do not fabricate competitor analysis — base it on what pages of this type typically cover and what is commonly missing in this service category.
📋 Outline Requirements
DEPTH RULE: The pillar page stays broad and scannable. Each major section should be scoped in 100–250 words at outline stage — depth belongs on the sub-pillar pages, not here.
1️⃣ SEO Metadata
•	SEO Title — 50–60 characters
•	Meta Description — 150–160 characters
•	URL slug
•	H1
2️⃣ Semantic Entity Map
List entities the pillar page should cover:
•	Core service entities and sub-service names
•	Tools / platforms used across sub-services
•	Methods / frameworks that apply across the whole pillar
•	Common client problems this pillar solves
•	Metrics or outcomes relevant across sub-services
•	Related questions a prospective client would ask before hiring
3️⃣ Entity & Sub-Pillar Relationship Map
Map how the pillar and its sub-pillars connect — this is a hub-and-spoke map, not a causal troubleshooting chain.
•	For each sub-pillar, identify what problem it solves and how it supports the others
•	Show how the sub-pillars work together as a system (e.g. Facebook Management → audience data → informs Instagram Management targeting)
•	Format: Pillar → Sub-Pillar A ↔ Sub-Pillar B ↔ Sub-Pillar C, noting any real dependencies between them
Example for Social Media Management:
Social Media Management (pillar) → Facebook Management + Instagram Management + Pinterest Management + LinkedIn Management, with Facebook/Instagram sharing Meta ad account data and creative assets
💡  This map should directly inform the internal linking architecture in Section 12 — do not treat it as a purely conceptual exercise.

4️⃣ Sub-Pillar / Service Directory
For each sub-pillar / cluster page linked from this pillar, provide:
•	Sub-pillar name and URL

5️⃣ Recommended Pillar Page Structure
Create a detailed H2/H3 outline following the standard pillar framework. For each H2 include: heading, what it should cover, suggested word count, retrieval block note, and entity terms to include.
Recommended sections for a pillar page:
•	Hero / Introduction — what the umbrella service is and who it's for
•	Quick answer — what this service includes at a glance
•	Sub-Pillar / Service Directory (see Section 4)
•	Why this service matters / the cost of not having it
•	Process / methodology — how the agency delivers this service
•	What's included — deliverables overview across sub-services
•	Pricing overview (placeholder structure, tiered or custom-quote framing)
•	Proof / results / why choose us
•	Comparison table (in-house vs. agency, or tier comparison)
•	FAQs
•	Final CTA
PILLAR-SPECIFIC
Stat / Number Opportunity Flag
For each major section, note whether it warrants a quantified claim (a specific number, percentage, or range) rather than a vague qualifier.
✅  Flag: Stat opportunity — Yes / No
✅  If Yes, note the direction and required source type (internal client data, published platform benchmark, industry report)
✅  Never invent a statistic to fill this flag — flag No if nothing verifiable is available
Front-Loading Directive
Roughly 44% of AI-cited content is pulled from the first third of a page — for a pillar page, that means the Hero/Intro and Quick Answer sections must state clearly, in the first few sentences, what the service is and what sub-services it includes.
✅  Confirm the Hero/Intro states the full scope of the service (all sub-pillars implied or named) before any persuasive copy
✅  Do not bury the service directory below multiple sections of marketing copy — it should appear early
Listicle / Format Priority Flag
Flag which sections should use list, table, or grid format instead of prose, since AI systems prioritize structured formats.
✅  Service Directory: always structured as a list or grid, never prose
✅  Process/methodology: numbered list
✅  Pricing overview: table if tiered, list if custom-quote
✅  Comparison section: table
6️⃣ Retrieval Blocks per Major Section
For each major H2, flag whether a retrieval block is needed. A retrieval block is a 30–60 word standalone answer that:
•	Directly answers what the section covers (e.g. "What does social media management include?")
•	Requires NO surrounding context to be understood
•	Uses subject + predicate structure — no vague openers
•	Can be independently extracted and cited by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude
For each flagged section, provide the question it answers, the format (definition/list/process/comparison), and a 1-sentence direction for the answer.
7️⃣ Direct Answer Blocks
Suggest 2-4 page-level direct answer blocks for featured snippets and AI extraction (e.g. "What is included in [service]?", "How much does [service] cost?"). For each: question, 40-60 word answer direction, placement.
8️⃣ Definition Box
Suggest whether a definition box is needed for the core service term. If yes: term to define, short definition direction, placement (immediately after Hero/Intro).
9️⃣ Table / Structured Content Ideas
Suggest at least 2 structured elements appropriate for a pillar page:
•	Service comparison table (what's included per tier or per sub-service)
•	In-house vs. agency comparison
•	Sub-pillar directory grid (see Section 4)
•	Process/timeline table
For each, provide: table title, columns, why it helps the reader decide.
🔟 Information Gain Opportunities
List 5-8 unique insights competing agency pages are unlikely to cover — specific to how this business actually delivers the service, not generic industry claims.
💡  At least 1 information gain item should appear per major section. A pillar page that reads like every competitor's page is a positioning failure, not just an SEO one.
1️⃣1️⃣ FAQ Outline

1️⃣2️⃣ Internal Linking Architecture
This is expanded beyond a standard article's internal links, since a pillar page's core job is routing traffic through the cluster.
•	Pillar → each sub-pillar: anchor text and placement for every sub-pillar link (from Section 4 directory, plus any additional contextual links within body copy)
•	Sub-pillar → pillar: confirm each sub-pillar page should link back to this pillar (breadcrumb-style or contextual)

•	Links to non-cluster pages: pricing page, portfolio/case studies, contact/quote page
1️⃣3️⃣ External Source Suggestions
Suggest 1-2 authoritative external sources if relevant (platform documentation, industry benchmark reports) — pillar pages typically need fewer external citations than blog content, since authority here comes primarily from proof and internal structure.

1️⃣4️⃣ CTA Strategy
Unlike a blog article's single conclusion, a pillar page needs multiple conversion points.
•	Primary CTA (usually after Hero/Intro and again at page end) — direction and button copy angle
•	Secondary CTAs after high-intent sections (Pricing, Proof/Results) — softer or alternate offer (e.g. free audit, consultation)
•	CTA variant notes: what changes between first-time visitors vs. comparison-stage visitors
PILLAR-SPECIFIC
1️⃣5️⃣ Schema Markup Suggestions
Recommend structured data types for a pillar/service page — different from a blog article's schema needs.
•	Service schema for the pillar and each sub-service
•	Organization schema (parent brand entity)
•	BreadcrumbList schema (pillar ↔ sub-pillar hierarchy)
•	FAQPage schema if the FAQ section is included
•	Whether author/reviewer credentials are warranted (usually less critical on service pages than blog content, but relevant if a named specialist owns the service)
